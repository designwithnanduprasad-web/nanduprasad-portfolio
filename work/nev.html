NEV Sonic

Seven primary images and a twelve-module A+ page for a mini massage gun. The job: make a small black gun stand out at thumbnail size, then answer every buyer question before they scroll away.

NEV
Product

01The brief

On a search page, every massage gun is a black gun on white. NEV Sonic’s real differences (eight heads, six speeds, under 42 dB, USB-C, small enough to pack) were invisible at that size.

The listing had to make those differences obvious in one swipe, without turning into a spec sheet.

02Primary images

Seven squares, one idea each, in the order a buyer asks: what do I get, which head is for what, how strong is it, is it for me, what’s inside, will it travel.

swipe →

03A+ content

Below the fold, the A+ page tells the longer story: the heads in use on real bodies, the six speeds as a scale you can read, quiet and charging as proof, then recovery moments for gym-goers, desk workers and older users, ending on a comparison table and what’s in the box.

04Design decisions

  1. Make the heads the hero

    Eight heads is the biggest difference from cheaper guns, so they appear in the main image and get their own guide, each mapped to the body part it’s for.

  2. Turn specs into feelings

    “42 dB” sits on a scale between falling leaves and normal conversation, and every speed is named by what it does, from “gentle wake-up rub” to “maximum deep-tissue relief”.

  3. Show every buyer

    The lifestyle images move from gym to office to sofa to bedroom, so the gun reads as everyday relief, not just athlete gear.

  4. Close the comparison

    The A+ page ends by answering the question buyers ask last: why this gun over the others, and exactly what comes in the box.
